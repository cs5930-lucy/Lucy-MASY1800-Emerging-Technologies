# Criteria and decision rationale

This is an operational interpretation of the assignment, not an invented quotation from a lecture. Four criteria distinguish new capability, breadth, societal pathway and constraints. A useful local gain does not by itself establish any higher significance label.

| Criterion | Meaning | Evidence and present assessment | Challenge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 capability | New reusable capability, with a technical comparator | Supported by S8; prompt-specified language tasks without separate task-specific weight updates | Uneven benchmarks and task limitations constrain scope |
| C2 breadth | Reusable mechanism in materially different activities | Supported at a screening level by S1 writing and S6 software construction | Two domains are a convention, not proof of universal reach |
| C3 societal pathway | Capability could persistently change production, knowledge access or institutions | Partial: some upstream task links have evidence; later institutional links are inference | Task gains may stay bounded or get absorbed without large durable effects |
| C4 constraints | Dependencies and failure conditions examined | Partial: S4, S7 and S9 expose reliability, integration and measurement problems | Infrastructure sustainability and equitable access remain unknown |

Incremental means a supported existing-process improvement without an established new capability class. Breakthrough requires credible C1 support. Potentially Promethean requires C1 and credible C2 support plus a specific, conditional C3 pathway with evidence for its upstream links and visible C4 bottlenecks. A definitive realized-impact claim requires more. Promotion, novelty and investment are excluded from the evidence rule.

Preferred judgment: potentially Promethean, provisional, with moderate to low confidence in that upper possibility and high confidence in the existence of demonstrated capabilities. Breakthrough remains the evidentiary floor. This does not assert inevitability, a date, net benefit or local adoption permission.

Pathway for scrutiny: reusable text and code generation may lower some knowledge-production costs; wider access to those activities could change how organizations create and coordinate work; durable changes could eventually affect institutions. Only the upstream task links are directly supported by this packet. Verification effort, uneven outcomes, access, infrastructure and governance may break the later links. Lower-cost misleading content could also harm information institutions; this is a risk scenario informed by S4 rather than a measured outcome here.

For Lucy's proposed application, broad significance should carry limited weight: it supports monitoring and an affordable learning option. Approved source matching, human approval, accepted-output quality and total effort should decide whether this particular workflow is useful. Smaller reliable savings may be sufficient. The original 80% goal is neither an achieved result nor a proven minimum threshold.
